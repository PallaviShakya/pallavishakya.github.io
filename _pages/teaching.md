My classrooms have ranged from a dirt floor in rural Nepal to a UC Davis lecture hall to a terminal window someone was afraid to touch. 
What carried across all three was the same handful of things.

<div class="principles">

  <div class="principle">
    <span class="head">Start where the learner is</span>
    <span class="line">In rural Nepal, where women had inherited generations of silence about their own bodies, I taught the reproductive system with chalk and marbles instead of a lecture.</span>
  </div>

  <div class="principle">
    <span class="head">Learning by building</span>
    <span class="line">People understand a thing once they have made it themselves, whether that is a bamboo polytunnel or their own analysis in R.</span>
  </div>

  <div class="principle">
    <span class="head">Mentor in both directions</span>
    <span class="line">I have taught undergraduates, PhD students, postdocs and PIs, and the job is the same in every direction: make the next step feel possible.</span>
  </div>

  <div class="principle">
    <span class="head">Permission to try</span>
    <span class="line">The barrier is rarely the material; it is whether someone feels allowed to try, break something, and ask again.</span>
  </div>

</div>
